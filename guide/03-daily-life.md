# Daily life and public spaces

[Home](../README.md) · [Phrasebook](10-phrasebook.md)

| Setting | Practical etiquette |
| --- | --- |
| Station platform | Queue at the markings and stand clear of the doors while passengers exit. Follow operator announcements and avoid rushing closing doors. |
| Train or bus | Keep your voice low, avoid phone calls, move bags away from doors, and offer priority seats to people who need them. Follow signs for designated cars and accessibility spaces. |
| Escalators and stairs | Follow posted directions and avoid walking on escalators where the operator instructs passengers to stand. Leave clear routes for people with mobility needs. |
| Sidewalk, market or tourist site | Pull aside before stopping to look at a map or taking a photo. Use designated eating areas when a market or venue asks you to. Follow local signage for smoking and litter. |
| Store or clinic | Use the queue or ticket system. Follow instructions at the register and use a payment tray when provided. Confirm whether bags, payment types or appointments have special rules. |
| Photos and video | Check signs inside shops, temples and event spaces. Ask before photographing identifiable people, and ask separately before sharing images if that is unclear. |
| Shared building | Keep corridors clear, keep noise down when neighbors are likely to be resting, and use common areas according to building rules. Check the visitor, bicycle and delivery policies. |
| Waste | Use the municipality’s collection calendar and your building’s collection point. Check how it handles plastics, cans, bottles, cardboard, batteries and bulky items. |

## Moving in and handling problems

Ask management for the local waste calendar and for the building’s collection area, bicycle registration and guest rules. Use the municipality’s guidance if a bag was left behind. If a neighbor raises a concern, ask for the time and specific issue so you can check it. For a repeated issue, contact the building manager with dates and practical details.

For paperwork, record the office name, required documents and reservation details. If Japanese is difficult, ask for plain Japanese or language support in advance. Tokyo’s resident consultation service lists available languages and contact hours.

## Emergencies and preparedness

In Japan, call **119** for fire or an ambulance and **110** for an urgent police response. Give your address, nearest landmark and what happened. Find your municipality’s hazard maps and designated evacuation locations before an emergency. At work, confirm the assembly point and follow the site emergency instructions.
