## Behaviors that often make interactions easier

| Situation | Often appreciated | Often causes friction | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An appointment or plan | Arriving at the agreed time or sending a realistic update | Arriving late without a message or canceling after someone has booked | How early the venue expects you |
| Shared public space | Keeping paths clear and managing noise or belongings | Blocking an exit, loud calls on a train, or leaving rubbish behind | Signs and operator guidance |
| A colleague helps you | Thanking them and telling them what happened afterward | Repeatedly asking for help without context or follow-up | Their preferred communication channel |
| Someone invites you | A clear reply and advance notice of dietary needs | A no-show or letting an RSVP lapse | Whether you can bring someone else |
| A disagreement | Showing the specific point and giving the person room to answer | Publicly blaming someone or using embarrassment to win agreement | The urgency and who has decision authority |
| Photos and personal information | Asking before recording, posting or sharing names | Posting someone’s image or office material without permission | Whether the venue also restricts photography |
| A mistake you made | Acknowledging the effect and giving a concrete correction | Delaying disclosure or making a promise you cannot keep | Who needs the update, and by when |

These are practical defaults. People's preferences vary with their relationship, age, region, workplace and circumstances. A quiet commuter may enjoy loud conversation at a concert, and a coworker may prefer very direct feedback during a design review.

## Common ideas in context

- **Meiwaku (迷惑, めいわく):** Inconvenience or trouble caused to someone. On a packed train, move your bag out of the aisle. At work, alert the next person when your delay changes their schedule.
- **Wa (和, わ):** Harmony. In a group discussion, give others a chance to explain their constraints. Raise material defects and safety issues explicitly.
- **Omotenashi (おもてなし):** Hospitality or attentive service. As a guest, acknowledge assistance and follow the host’s arrangements.
- **Honne and tatemae (本音・建前, ほんね・たてまえ):** Private feelings and publicly appropriate positions. These words describe a possible difference in expression. If a decision matters, ask what was decided, who will confirm it, and when.
- **Kūki o yomu (空気を読む, くうきをよむ):** Pick up the mood or social cues. In a meeting, notice whether people want more context. For a work instruction, confirm directly if the meaning affects the outcome.

## What people say they want at work

In Japan’s Cabinet Office August 2025 national life survey, 2,729 people were in the survey base for a multiple-response question about their ideal job. Stable income was selected by **61.6%**, balance with private life by **54.9%**, and enjoyable work by **51.6%**. The same item included work that made use of expertise (**35.8%**) and high income (**21.4%**). Respondents could choose multiple answers, so the percentages do not sum to 100%. The answers describe the surveyed preferences for this question. Ask individual colleagues what matters to them. [Cabinet Office summary, question 18](https://survey.gov-online.go.jp/202509/r07/r07-life/gairyaku.pdf)

## Learning someone’s likes and dislikes

Use low-pressure questions about the setting. “Is there a way you prefer to receive progress updates?” gives your colleague a concrete choice. “Would you rather have lunch here or somewhere quieter?” works with a friend. For broader attitudes, check the survey year, sample, question wording and response options before drawing a conclusion.
