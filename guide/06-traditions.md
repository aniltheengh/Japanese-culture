## Seasonal customs

| Occasion | What you may see | How to participate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| お正月, New Year | Family gatherings, seasonal meals and 初詣, the first shrine or temple visit of the year | Check holiday opening hours. Follow local instructions and queues during a visit. |
| 花見, cherry blossom viewing | People walk or gather for picnics where the trees bloom | Use the park’s designated areas, avoid damaging trees, and clean up afterward. |
| Golden Week | Several national holidays close together and increased domestic travel | Confirm schedules and reservations ahead of time. |
| お盆, Obon | Family observances and remembrance of ancestors, sometimes with local Bon dances | Dates vary by area. Ask hosts about their own customs and check business closures. |
| 祭り, local festivals | Processions, food stalls, music or dancing, depending on the event | Keep processional paths clear and ask organizers before joining a group activity. |
| 忘年会・新年会, year-end or New Year gatherings | Social meals for friends, clubs or colleagues | Check the RSVP, budget and cancellation deadline. |

## Shrines and temples

Shinto shrines and Buddhist temples serve different traditions. People may visit either or both for family occasions, seasonal customs, prayer or tourism. At many shrines a common worship sequence includes bowing and clapping. At Buddhist temples people commonly join their hands quietly. Follow posted instructions or observe the people around you because practices vary by site. Respect restricted areas and photography rules. [JNTO shrine and temple guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/shrine-and-temple-traditions/)

## Regions and communities

Japan has regional dialects, food traditions and local histories. Learn about the Indigenous Ainu through Upopoy and the National Ainu Museum. Okinawa’s Ryukyu heritage includes its own history, music and food traditions. Regional dishes offer a practical way to start a conversation when traveling or sharing a meal. City, suburban and rural life also differ, and traditions vary between families. [National Ainu Museum](https://ainu-upopoy.go.jp/en/museum/) · [Official Okinawa guide to Ryukyu heritage](https://visitokinawajapan.com/travel-inspiration/local-culture-in-world-heritage-okinawa/) · [MAFF regional cuisines](https://www.maff.go.jp/e/policies/market/k_ryouri/index.html)

## Contemporary life and interests

Some ways to explore modern Japan are local sports, music, fashion, cinema, manga, games, libraries, museums, theater and neighborhood arts events. A person may enjoy any combination of these or have entirely different interests. Ask what they watch, read or do on weekends. If invited to a club, check how to join and what contribution is expected. The Statistics Bureau’s handbook is a starting point for changes in households, work and social conditions. [Statistics Bureau handbook](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/handbook/)

In the Statistics Bureau’s 2021 leisure survey, **52.7%** reported watching movies outside a theater, **42.9%** playing games on phones, consoles or similar devices, and **36.8%** reading manga. These figures measure participation in specific activities during the survey period. The survey includes residents aged 10 and older, and interests vary by age and person. [Statistics Bureau leisure results, table 4-3](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/shakai/2021/pdf/activities2021.pdf) · [Survey scope](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/shakai/2021/gaiyo.htm)

## History, arts and education

Japan’s National Diet Library offers a starting point for the political changes from the opening of the country and the Meiji period to the postwar years. Cultural practices such as tea ceremony, flower arranging, calligraphy and bonsai have their own histories and schools. At a demonstration or workshop, ask whether you may handle materials or take photographs. [National Diet Library modern history archive](https://www.ndl.go.jp/modern/e/) · [JNTO cultural heritage guide](https://faq.japan-travel.jnto.go.jp/en/guide/japans-cultural-heritage/)

The school year generally runs from April through March. Compulsory education covers elementary and junior high school. For a child entering school, confirm enrollment procedures, supplies, attendance and school communications with the local authority and school. The education ministry publishes a multilingual school-enrollment guide for families. [MEXT guide for foreign students and guardians](https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/nihongo_kyoiku/kyoiku/handbook/pdf/en_zensho.pdf)
