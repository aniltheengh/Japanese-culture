# Food, hospitality and bathing

[Home](../README.md) · [Phrasebook](10-phrasebook.md)

## Dining out and at home

- Check whether you should wait to be seated. At a group meal, take the host’s lead on starting and sharing food. いただきます before eating and ごちそうさまでした afterward are common expressions of appreciation.
- Rest chopsticks on the holder or beside your dish. Avoid placing them upright in rice or passing food directly from one pair of chopsticks to another, both of which evoke funeral customs. Use serving utensils for shared dishes when available.
- For allergies or dietary restrictions, explain the ingredient and whether shared preparation is a concern. Broth, sauces and garnishes can contain ingredients that are not visible. For a serious allergy, ask staff to confirm the ingredient and shared-equipment risk before ordering.
- In some izakaya, an appetizer called お通し may carry a charge. Ask about it if unsure. In a group, check whether the bill will be split equally or by order. Ordinary restaurant tipping is generally uncommon. Pay any service charge shown by the venue.
- At a home’s 玄関, remove outdoor shoes before stepping up. Follow the host on slippers, and remove slippers before entering tatami rooms. Some places provide separate toilet slippers. When leaving, thank the host and send a short follow-up if appropriate.

## Gifts and events

A small consumable gift can suit a home visit when you know the host can use it. お土産 often refers to souvenirs brought back from travel. A gift at work can be optional, and clients or regulated workplaces may have gift policies. Ask before bringing something expensive. For weddings and funerals, confirm the envelope type, dress code and expectations with the organizer or someone familiar with the event. Customs differ by relationship, region and religious tradition.

## Bathing and staying overnight

At a communal bath, wash and rinse before entering. Keep towels out of the bath, tie up long hair so it stays out of the water, and leave phones and cameras in the appropriate storage area. Check the establishment’s tattoo and swimsuit policies in advance. Some facilities offer private baths, while others have rules about covering tattoos.

At a ryokan, check meal times, footwear boundaries, room instructions and whether yukata can be worn outside the room. At a shared accommodation, confirm quiet hours, kitchen cleanup and bathroom schedules.
