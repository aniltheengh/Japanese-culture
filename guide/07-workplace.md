## What managers and coworkers may value

| Behavior | What it looks like | Common friction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliability | Arrive ready, track due dates, and tell people when a commitment is at risk | Surprising the downstream team on the due date |
| Accuracy | Use the current inputs, check results, label revisions and record assumptions | Releasing unreviewed work or using an obsolete reference |
| Ownership | Report errors, explain their effect, and help correct them | Concealing a mistake or guessing who caused it |
| Teamwork | Share changes with affected colleagues and ask who owns the handoff | Changing a shared file or process without coordination |
| Judgment | Ask early when an ambiguity could cause rework or a safety risk | Waiting for complete certainty before flagging a significant issue |
| Improvement | Describe a problem, propose a small trial, and measure its effect | Changing a validated process before its owner approves the trial |
| Respect for time | Keep messages focused, honor agreed working hours, and arrange coverage before leave | Leaving handovers unclear or assuming colleagues are always available |

Ask your supervisor which behaviors matter for your role and how they are evaluated. Some teams prefer a short daily report, while others want only blockers and milestone updates.

## Reporting, informing and consulting

**報連相 (ほうれんそう, hōrensō)** is shorthand for 報告 (report), 連絡 (inform) and 相談 (consult).

- **Report progress:** “Six of eight drawings are checked. The two remaining drawings need the vendor’s dimensions.”
- **Inform affected people:** “The vendor revision may move the nozzle location. Please hold the affected isometric until we confirm.”
- **Consult on a decision:** “We can issue unaffected sheets today or wait and issue the full set tomorrow. Which plan should we use?”

Agree on which issues require an immediate call and which can wait for the regular update. JETRO encourages employers to make instructions clear and improve communication on both sides. Use written confirmation for details that affect delivery, safety or cost.

## Decisions, seniority and disagreements

**根回し (ねまわし, nemawashi)** is advance discussion with stakeholders. **稟議 (りんぎ, ringi)** is an internal approval process used in some organizations. In a project, the people consulted may differ from the person who can approve a change. Share options and impacts before the review when useful. Record the actual decision, approver, due date and outstanding conditions.

Some teams address supervisors by title and have formal reporting lines. Others use family name + さん across levels. Observe local conventions and ask who can approve your specific work. When you disagree, lead with the relevant fact and consequence. “The vendor drawing shows a different nozzle position, so the spool may clash with the platform” gives people something concrete to check. Raise urgent hazards immediately through the site procedure.

## Office, meetings and client visits

- **First week:** Ask about working hours, dress, communication channels, lunch, timekeeping, file locations and approvals. Confirm who can answer when your supervisor is away.
- **Meetings:** Read the agenda, bring the current document, and send a short record with decisions, action owners and dates. Acknowledge open items explicitly.
- **Client visits:** Confirm names, roles, room and purpose. Follow the host’s lead on seating, business cards and formality. When exchanging cards, present yours so the recipient can read it and handle the received card respectfully.
- **Mistakes:** Contain the impact, inform the owner, correct through the approved route, and document the change. An apology is most useful alongside the correction.
- **Continuous improvement:** 改善 (かいぜん, kaizen) means improvement. Toyota’s production system is one example of a company-specific approach. In a design office, a small approved drawing-checklist trial may be more relevant than adopting a production method wholesale.
