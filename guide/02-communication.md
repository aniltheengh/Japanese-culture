## Greetings and names

- A brief bow or nod is a common greeting in a first meeting, at reception or when thanking someone. Follow the host’s lead on handshakes and formality.
- Start with family name + さん in most everyday professional introductions unless the person or workplace uses a title or another convention. When addressing a customer, follow your team’s guidance on titles and names, including how to refer to your own colleagues.
- Use polite です／ます Japanese with strangers, customers and new colleagues. Use more formal set phrases in customer email when the context calls for them. Clear wording, a concrete request and an explicit due date help the reader act.
- お疲れさまです is a common internal greeting or acknowledgment. お世話になっております is a common opener to an external business contact when a working relationship exists. Use 初めまして for a first personal introduction.

## Replies you may hear

| Japanese | Reading / romaji | What to do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| はい | はい · hai | It may acknowledge hearing or understanding. If agreement matters, confirm the specific action. |
| 大丈夫です | だいじょうぶです · daijōbu desu | Context can mean “it’s fine” or “no, thank you.” Ask again if the result affects an order or safety. |
| ちょっと難しいです | ちょっとむずかしいです · chotto muzukashii desu | It can signal a difficulty or a polite refusal. Ask whether another date or scope would work. |
| 検討します | けんとうします · kentō shimasu | The person will consider it. Confirm an owner or follow-up date if needed. |
| そうですね | そうですね · sō desu ne | It can acknowledge your point, express agreement or give time to think. Listen for the decision that follows. |

## Ask for clarity without losing momentum

Repeat back the part that changes the work. For example, after a verbal assignment, say **確認ですが、金曜日までにPDFを提出すればよろしいですか。** (“Just to confirm, should I submit the PDF by Friday?”). Confirm what “by Friday” means if a meeting or transmittal depends on it. In a noisy office or online meeting, ask the other person to send the drawing number or unfamiliar term in chat.

To interrupt, open with **すみません、今お時間よろしいですか。** If you need to disagree, identify the drawing, instruction or assumption and explain its consequence. If you missed a deadline, acknowledge the missed commitment and add the next delivery time. For a minor inconvenience, すみません is often enough. For a missed customer commitment, follow the company’s escalation and apology process.

## Phone and messages

At work, say your name and team, state the purpose, and confirm names or numbers before hanging up. In chat, put the issue and request in the first lines. For nonurgent messages after hours, use the channel and timing your team expects. In a group thread, tag only people whose input or action is needed.
